Basket or oven-style air fryer: what should you compare?

The distinction, without the marketing

Basket units are a single cooking chamber with a drawer. Oven-style units are a small countertop cavity with shelves and usually a door. The comparison is not which is better; it is which fits how you cook and how much counter and cupboard you have.

The comparison axes

AxisBasketOven-style
Counter footprintUsually smallerUsually larger, and needs door-swing clearance
Batch shapeOne deep batchSeveral shallow trays at once
Stirring/shakingNeeded for even results in most basket unitsLess needed with multiple shelves, more needed for rotation
CleaningOne drawer, often dishwasher-safe partsShelves, walls and a door seal
StorageDrawer must be removable to store compactlyBulkier single volume
VisibilityOpaque — you open it to lookUsually a window, which costs heat each time you look

What to write down before you compare models

  • The largest batch you actually cook, in weight or in pieces — not the batch you imagine cooking.
  • The counter depth and height available, including clearance for a door to open.
  • Where it will live between uses, and whether you will lift it.
  • How many parts go into a dishwasher, and whether you own a dishwasher.
  • Whether you would use it for anything other than air frying.
