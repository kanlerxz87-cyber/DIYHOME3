How do you read an Amazon listing without being led by it?

Which parts of a product page are seller-authored, which are Amazon-computed, and which are neither.

By DIYHOME3 Editorial Desk Automated research and drafting; pending named human review Published 2026-11-20 Season Black Friday / Cyber Monday 2026 Editorial standards

Three different authors share one page

A product detail page mixes text written by the seller, numbers computed by Amazon, and content contributed by shoppers. They carry very different evidential weight, and the page does not label them for you.

Who wrote what

ElementAuthored byHow to treat it
Title and subheadThe sellerA marketing claim. Useful for what the product is called and what it says it does; not evidence that it does it
Bullet points and descriptionThe sellerClaims. Compare against the specification table where one exists
Star rating and ratings countAmazon, computed from shopper submissionsA real aggregate, but of shopper opinion, not of measured performance
"Amazon’s Choice" badgeAmazonAmazon states it highlights highly rated, well-priced products available to ship immediately. It is a merchandising signal, not a test result
"X bought in past month"AmazonA sales-volume signal for that listing, not a quality signal
Price and any discountAmazon, from the seller’s offerAlways read the anchor label next to it
"Typical price"Amazon, defined as the 90-day median paid, excluding limited-time promotionsA backward-looking median, not a promise
"List Price"Amazon, from a manufacturer, supplier or seller suggestionAmazon states list prices may not reflect the prevailing market price
Ships from / Sold byAmazonTells you whether the seller is Amazon or a third party, which matters for returns and support
Status of this article

Definitions of "Typical Price" and "List Price" were read verbatim from the listings themselves on 2026-11-20.

The two anchor labels are not interchangeable

A percentage discount is only as meaningful as its anchor. Amazon renders two different anchors and defines both on the page. A "Typical price" anchor is a 90-day median of what customers actually paid. A "List Price" anchor is a suggested retail figure that Amazon itself says may not reflect the prevailing market price. The same 26% off means materially different things against those two baselines.

Observed · point-in-time record

Observed on 2026-11-20: B08FKW9JRM and B0BSKQW2T2 both rendered a "Typical price" anchor, while B08DY9HRWD rendered a "List Price" anchor alongside the note "Exclusive Prime price" and the prompt "Join Prime to buy this item at $17.74". The observed prices are therefore not directly comparable to each other without also comparing their anchors and their eligibility conditions. S06 S07 S08

A reading order that works

  • Read the specification table before the bullets, if the listing has one.
  • Read "Ships from / Sold by" before the price, so you know whose offer you are looking at.
  • Read the price anchor label before the discount percentage.
  • Check whether the price is gated behind a membership.
  • Read the returns line, especially near a seasonal window.
  • Only then read the star rating, and read it as an opinion aggregate.
