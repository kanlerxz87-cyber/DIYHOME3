FTC endorsement guidance applied to this site

The Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255, were revised in 2023. Three sections shape this site directly, and each one removed something from the copy.

§255.1(a) — honest experience

Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions, findings, beliefs, or experience of the endorser. Furthermore, an endorsement may not convey any express or implied representation that would be deceptive if made directly by the advertiser. 16 CFR §255.1(a) S10

What this removed: every first-person experience claim. There is no "I tested this", no "we found", no "in our kitchen", no "after three months of use". No human has used any product in this register for this site, so the copy asserts no experience at all. This is enforced mechanically: tools/verify.mjs scans every built page for a fixed list of experience phrases and fails the build on a match.

What it cost: persuasiveness. Experience copy converts better than criteria copy, and this site gives that up deliberately. The guides are written as frameworks a reader can apply, rather than as recommendations they can accept.

§255.3 — expert endorsements

Where an advertisement represents, directly or by implication, that the endorser is an expert on the endorsed product, the endorser’s qualifications must actually give them that expertise. S10

What this removed: any expert byline. The author of record is DIYHOME3 Editorial Desk, labelled on every article as Automated research and drafting; pending named human review. No credentials are claimed, no Person structured data is emitted, and no biography is invented. Assigning a real named reviewer is the first open action on the Flagged for Review page.

§255.5 — material connections

A material connection between an endorser and the marketer must be clearly and conspicuously disclosed. S10 Amazon’s own guidance restates the standard for affiliate links: the disclosure must be clear, conspicuous, placed near the link or product review, somewhere customers will notice easily — they should not have to hunt for it. Where the FTC and Amazon requirements differ

They overlap but they are not the same, and satisfying one does not satisfy the other. Amazon’s Operating Agreement §5 mandates a specific sentence. The FTC does not mandate wording; it mandates that the disclosure be clear and conspicuous in context. Amazon’s guidance adds a link-level disclosure on top of the site-level statement, which is the FTC standard restated for affiliates. This site implements all three: the exact sentence, above the fold, and repeated next to each link.

Consumer reviews — a related rule this site avoids entirely

The FTC also regulates the use of consumer reviews and testimonials, and Amazon’s Operating Agreement separately notes that content posted on amazon.com must abide by Amazon’s Community Guidelines, including the prohibition on customer reviews created, edited or removed in exchange for compensation. S01 S11 This site quotes no customer review at all, which removes the question rather than answering it.
